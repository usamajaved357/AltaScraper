## SUPPLEMENTS (massively tightened January 2026)

**Status:** GATED — among the most heavily enforced categories on Amazon

**What changed in 2026:** Amazon now requires ALL dietary supplement sellers to provide:
- Third-party cGMP verification from one of ONLY 7 approved labs (Certified Laboratories, Eurofins, Intertek, Mérieux NutriSciences, NSF International, SGS, UL)
- No other labs accepted, even if otherwise reputable
- 90 days from notification to comply or listings deactivated
- Every ingredient claim on the detail page must EXACTLY match the Supplement Facts Panel — name, weight, presentation
- "High-risk" subcategories (bodybuilding, joint health, sexual enhancement, sports nutrition, weight management) need additional NSF/ANSI 173-2023 testing

**Prohibited products (cannot list at all):**
- Products in FDA recall
- Products containing prohibited ingredients (ephedra, DMAA, BMPEA, synthetic stimulants)
- Products with undeclared pharmaceutical ingredients
- Products making disease claims ("cures cancer," "treats diabetes")
- Single/double-pill packs of sexual enhancement or weight loss supplements (since Dec 2021)
- Any product an FDA warning letter has been issued against

**Required docs (US):**
- cGMP certificate from one of the 7 approved TIC providers
- Supplement Facts Panel (the "single source of truth")
- FDA-compliant labelling (product name, quantity, ingredients, manufacturer)
- Third-party lab testing (ISO 17025 accredited)
- For high-risk categories: NSF/ANSI 173-2023 testing

**Required docs (UK):**
- FSA/MHRA compliance
- Proper labelling with all ingredients
- Novel Food registration if applicable

**Trigger keywords:** dietary supplement, vitamin, mineral, herbal, botanical, amino acid, protein powder, collagen, pre-workout, fat burner, testosterone booster, probiotic, melatonin

**Claims trap:** A supplement claiming to TREAT or CURE a disease becomes an unapproved DRUG — instant restriction change. "Supports immune health" = OK. "Cures flu" = violation.

**Impact on arbitrage:** Effectively closed to arbitrage/dropshipping without access to the manufacturer's cGMP documentation and lab reports. You cannot resell supplements without the manufacturer's certification chain.

**Regulator:** FDA (US, DSHEA / 21 CFR 111/117) / FSA-MHRA (UK)

## CHILDREN'S PRODUCTS / TOYS (annual testing mandate since Sep 2025)

**Status:** GATED — annual compliance testing now mandatory

**The Direct Validation (DV) mandate (September 2025):**
- ALL children's toys require annual testing/document verification from an Amazon-approved TIC body
- Applies to US, Canada, and EU stores
- Products failing compliance are ASIN-removed, not just suppressed
- "Arbitrage and dropshipping in toys are likely unsustainable" — industry assessment

**Required testing/docs (US):**
- Children's Product Certificate (CPC) based on third-party lab testing from CPSC-accepted lab
- ASTM F963-23 testing (mechanical/physical safety, flammability, toxicology)
- Lead content testing (< 100ppm in accessible parts)
- Phthalates testing (< 0.1% for 8 specified phthalates)
- CPSIA tracking labels (manufacturer, date, batch)
- Small parts warning labels (choking hazard for under-3)
- Annual re-testing required

**Required testing/docs (UK/EU):**
- EN71-1: Mechanical & physical properties
- EN71-2: Flammability
- EN71-3: Migration of certain elements (toxic chemicals)
- EN62115 for electric/electronic toys
- UKCA marking (UK) / CE marking (EU)
- UK/EU Authorised Representative details on product
- REACH compliance (hazardous substances)
- Declaration of Conformity

**Scope trap:** Products NOT marketed as toys but used by children (children's furniture, clothing, baby products, children's jewelry) ALSO fall under CPSIA/CPC requirements. "Marketed to or primarily intended for children 12 and under" is the trigger, not the product type.

**Trigger keywords:** toy, children, kids, baby, toddler, infant, play, educational toy, plush, stuffed animal, action figure, building blocks, puzzle

**Impact on arbitrage:** Cannot resell without the manufacturer's CPC and lab reports. Annual re-testing means documentation must be current. Your Italian Brainrot plush toys (Selvora) are in this category.

**Regulator:** CPSC / CPSIA (US) / UK Product Safety (EN71/UKCA)

## WEAPONS / KNIVES (UK FBM ban is the critical one)

**Status:** PROHIBITED (most weapons) / HEAVILY RESTRICTED (knives)

**UK-specific (Offensive Weapons Act 2019 — your main market):**
- Since May 2022: ALL bladed articles requiring age verification are PROHIBITED from seller-fulfilled (FBM) orders on Amazon.co.uk
- This includes kitchen knives, chef's knives — not just weapons
- FBA is allowed for eligible bladed products ONLY (Amazon handles age verification at delivery)
- FBA exceptions limited to: (i) razor cartridges with <2mm exposed blade, (ii) ordinary cutlery sets with spoons/forks, (iii-iv) certain tools with proper age-restriction flags
- Must set "Customer Restriction Type" attribute for eligible bladed products
- 2024: zombie-style knives/machetes added to prohibited list
- 2025: ninja swords added to prohibited list
- Crime and Policing Act 2026: new National Knife Crime Coordination Centre targeting online sales

**Prohibited in UK (cannot sell under any circumstance):**
- Flick knives / switchblades / automatic knives
- Gravity knives
- Zombie knives / zombie-style machetes
- Ninja swords
- Butterfly knives / balisongs
- Push daggers
- Knuckledusters
- Disguised knives (belt buckle knives, pen knives designed as weapons)
- Sword canes
- Crossbows (additional restrictions under Crime and Policing Act 2026)

**US:**
- Firearms and ammunition: prohibited
- Certain knives (switchblades) restricted in many states
- Weapons marketed for violence: prohibited

**Trigger keywords:** knife, blade, sword, machete, dagger, combat, tactical knife, hunting knife, throwing knife, stiletto, switchblade, automatic knife, self-defense weapon, crossbow, knuckleduster

**Critical for your arbitrage:** If you're doing UK FBM (which you are), you CANNOT sell any bladed article that needs age verification — period. This is Amazon UK's implementation of the Offensive Weapons Act, not a suggestion. FBA is the only path, and only for the exempted categories.

**Regulator:** UK Home Office (Offensive Weapons Act 2019, Criminal Justice Act 1988) / ATF (US)

## CBD / HEMP (prohibited with concealment trap)

**Status:** PROHIBITED on Amazon (both US and UK)

**Amazon's position:** "Products containing cannabidiol (CBD) are prohibited" — oils, capsules, topicals, edibles, beauty products, all forms.

**The concealment trap (critically important for your checker):**
- Many sellers relabel CBD products as "hemp oil," "hemp extract," or "hemp oil extract" to bypass Amazon's filters
- These products often contain CBD but sellers cannot disclose cannabinoid content
- Amazon actively scans for concealment and suspends accounts
- If your checker sees "hemp extract" + health claims + no ingredient disclosure, it should flag as potential CBD concealment
- This is EXACTLY the Shee'lady Section 3 pattern: trying to sell a restricted product under a different name

**What IS allowed:**
- Hemp SEED oil (pressed from seeds only, contains no CBD/THC) — clearly labelled as food/cosmetic ingredient
- Hemp textiles, clothing, rope — industrial hemp products

**What is NOT allowed:**
- Any product containing CBD, THC, or hemp resin
- Any product marketed as CBD regardless of actual content
- Products making health claims related to cannabinoids

**UK additional:** CBD as a food supplement requires FSA Novel Food authorisation — few products have this

**Trigger keywords:** CBD, cannabidiol, hemp extract, hemp oil extract, full spectrum hemp, broad spectrum, THC, cannabinoid, cannabis, marijuana, hemp flower, hemp tincture, endocannabinoid

**Concealment detection keywords (flag as suspicious):** "hemp oil" + any health claim, "natural hemp," "hemp drops" with mg dosage listed, "1000mg hemp" (real hemp seed oil isn't dosed in mg like this)

**Regulator:** FDA (US, not yet regulated as supplement) / FSA (UK, Novel Food) / Amazon's own policy (stricter than law)

## LASERS (new July 2026 enforcement deadline)

**Status:** GATED (Class I-IIIa/IIIr ≤5mW) / PROHIBITED (Class IIIb and IV)

**The hard ceiling:** Maximum 5mW output power for any laser sold as a pointer or consumer product on Amazon. This is FDA's limit (21 CFR 1040.10) and Amazon enforces it strictly.

**New enforcement (May-July 2026):**
- Amazon policy effective May 19, 2026
- Non-compliant product removal deadline: July 3, 2026
- High-risk targets: 303 series laser pens, cat laser toys, "burning lasers"
- Must have test reports from accredited lab (ISO 17025) within past 12 months
- US listings require FDA confirmation with valid Accession Number

**Prohibited:**
- Class IIIb (3b) lasers: 5-500mW — prohibited without FDA variance (Amazon doesn't support variances)
- Class IV (4) lasers: >500mW — prohibited, period
- Any laser marketed as "burning" or capable of igniting materials
- Laser products marketed to treat acne or skin conditions without 510(k) = classified as professional-use medical device

**UK additional:**
- UKHSA/WHO: consumer laser products should be Class 1 or Class 2 only
- Class 3 and 4 lasers flagged by OPSS (Office for Product Safety and Standards)
- Multiple Amazon ASINs recalled by UK authorities for excessive laser power

**Required docs (if allowed class):**
- FDA Accession Number (US)
- Test report from accredited lab (power output verification)
- Proper laser class labelling per FDA 21 CFR 1040.10
- Safety features: key switch for Class IIIa, emission indicator

**Trigger keywords:** laser pointer, laser pen, 303 laser, burning laser, high power laser, class 3 laser, class 4 laser, class 3b laser, 50mw, 100mw, 500mw, 1000mw, 5w laser, green laser, blue laser, laser engraver (consumer), laser cat toy

**Cross-reference:** The laser + medical claims trap — a laser device claiming to treat acne, wrinkles, or pain becomes a Class II medical device requiring 510(k). Ties to medical device trigger.

**Regulator:** FDA CDRH (US, 21 CFR 1040.10) / UKHSA + OPSS (UK)

## PESTICIDES / BIOCIDES (the word-trigger category)

**Status:** GATED (registered products) / PROHIBITED (unregistered + certain chemicals)

**Why this category is unique:** It's triggered by WORDS more than products. A kitchen sponge claiming "antibacterial" gets flagged as a pesticide. Amazon's automated bots scan listings for pesticide-claim language and suppress them instantly, often without notice.

**The trigger word list (Amazon's automated detection):**
- Primary triggers: kills, kills 99.9%, antimicrobial, antibacterial, antifungal, anti-mold, disinfects, disinfectant, sanitizes, sanitizer
- Secondary triggers: repels, repellent, mildew-resistant, mold-resistant, germ-killing, bacteria-killing, pest control, insect repellent, bug killer, rodent repellent
- Even IMAGE triggers: pictures of bugs dying, germs being zapped, or "shield" graphics implying germ protection
- A+ content and bullet points are ALL scanned

**FIFRA classification (US law):**
- Pesticide: product intended to prevent/destroy/repel/mitigate any pest (including bacteria/viruses/fungi)
- Pesticide device: achieves same result without chemical substance (bug zappers, UV sanitizers, ultrasonic repellers)
- Pesticide claim: ANY product marketed with pesticidal language, even if it's not a pesticide

**Required (if legitimate pesticide):**
- EPA Registration Number (for chemical pesticides)
- EPA Establishment Number (for pesticide devices — facility, not product)
- Complete Pesticide Marking attribute in Seller Central
- State-level registration (varies by state)

**Required (UK):**
- HSE biocide registration
- UK Biocidal Products Regulation compliance

**Prohibited specifically:**
- Neonicotinoid pesticides for outdoor plant/crop use (since 2020 EPA directive)
- Products in broken packaging
- Products differing from EPA-approved label

**The false-positive problem (relevant to your claims screener):**
This is the EXACT Mil-Gear pattern: a non-pesticide product (gear oil) gets flagged because the listing contains "antimicrobial" or similar language. The fix is removing the language, not getting EPA registration. Your claims screener should catch these trigger words and warn BEFORE Amazon's bots do.

**Regulator:** EPA / FIFRA (US) / HSE (UK)

## FOOD / GROCERY

**Status:** GATED

**Requirements (US):**
- FDA Food Facility Registration
- Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA) compliance
- Proper labelling: nutrition facts, ingredients, allergens, best-by date
- Shelf life: must have sufficient remaining shelf life at FBA check-in
- Temperature-sensitive products: cold chain requirements
- No "homemade" food without proper licensing

**Requirements (UK):**
- FSA (Food Standards Agency) compliance
- Proper labelling: ingredients, allergens (14 major allergens), nutrition, origin
- Best Before / Use By dates clearly displayed
- Traceability (batch codes, manufacturer info)

**Specific prohibitions:**
- Raw milk (US)
- Unpasteurised products without proper labelling
- Products with undeclared allergens
- Products past expiry
- Alcohol-infused food above threshold

**Trigger keywords:** edible, consumable, food supplement, nutritional, organic food (needs USDA organic cert), keto, vegan (if making health claims)

**Regulator:** FDA / FSMA (US) / FSA (UK)

## LIGHTING / ENERGY PRODUCTS

**Status:** RESTRICTED (efficiency standards + specific bans)

**US (DOE):**
- Department of Energy efficiency standards for general service lamps
- Effective July 2023: incandescent and halogen bulbs below 45 lumens/watt effectively banned
- LED replacements must meet Energy Star or equivalent

**UK:**
- Since September 2021: halogen bulbs banned for general lighting
- Since September 2023: fluorescent tube (T8/T12) and compact fluorescent (CFL) banned
- Only LED/energy-efficient alternatives allowed
- UK Energy Labelling requirements for light sources

**Required docs:**
- Energy efficiency certification/rating
- UK energy label (for covered products)
- DOE compliance documentation (US)

**Trigger keywords:** incandescent bulb, halogen bulb, CFL, fluorescent tube, T8, T12

**Regulator:** DOE (US) / OPSS + UK Energy regulations

## REFRIGERANTS / OZONE-DEPLETING SUBSTANCES

**Status:** GATED (heavily regulated)

**US:**
- EPA Section 608: technician certification required to purchase refrigerants
- R-410A, R-134a, R-22: all regulated

- Sellers must verify buyer certification before shipping

**UK:**
- F-gas Regulation: phase-down of HFCs
- R-134a (common car AC refrigerant) restricted

**Trigger keywords:** refrigerant, R-134a, R-410A, R-22, freon, AC recharge, HVAC refrigerant, coolant gas

**Your specific relevance:** Your restricted-product checker already has R-134a as a STRONG keyword. This category affects automotive accessories (AC recharge kits).

**Regulator:** EPA Section 608 / AIM Act (US) / UK F-gas Regulation

## SURVEILLANCE / SPY EQUIPMENT

**Status:** RESTRICTED

**What's restricted:**
- Hidden cameras / spy cameras (especially disguised as everyday objects)
- Audio recording devices marketed for covert use
- GPS trackers marketed for covert surveillance
- Cell phone jammers: PROHIBITED (both US and UK)
- Wi-Fi jammers: PROHIBITED
- Devices marketed with "spy," "covert," "hidden," "undetectable"

**What's allowed:**
- Security cameras (clearly marketed as security, not spy)
- Baby monitors
- Dashcams
- GPS trackers for personal asset tracking (your own car/bag)

**The marketing line:** The product itself may be identical; it's the MARKETING that determines restriction. A camera sold as "home security" is fine; the same camera sold as "hidden nanny cam spy device" is restricted.

**Trigger keywords:** spy camera, hidden camera, covert recording, nanny cam spy, GPS tracker covert, cell phone jammer, signal jammer, bug detector, counter-surveillance, body-worn hidden camera

**Regulator:** FCC (US, jammers) / Ofcom (UK, jammers) / RIPA (UK, covert surveillance)

## CROSS-CUTTING NOTES

**The 2026 enforcement pattern:** Amazon is systematically moving from reactive (flag after listing) to proactive (require documentation before listing) across high-risk categories. Supplements, toys, and lasers all received new "prove it first" mandates in 2025-2026. Expect this pattern to extend to more categories.

**The arbitrage impact:** Several categories (supplements, children's toys) are now effectively closed to arbitrage/dropshipping because you cannot produce the manufacturer's certification chain. The product physically exists and is legal to sell — you just can't prove compliance without the manufacturer's cooperation.

**Your checker's honest limit:** This reference covers the landscape as of August 2026. It catches known restricted types and triggers. It CANNOT catch: new restrictions Amazon adds without notice, product-specific gating (a single ASIN restricted while the category is open), or brand gating (you can't sell Brand X's products regardless of category). The only truly current, per-ASIN answer is Seller Central's "Apply to Sell."
