How the Repricer works

A plain-English guide to what the Repricer does with your FBM listings: how it reads your suppliers, how it works out a price, and exactly what it will and will not send to Amazon. Written from the app's code as of 30 Sep 2026.

1What it is for

For listings you ship yourself (FBM) and buy from a supplier after a customer orders, the Repricer keeps three things right on Amazon, per SKU:

PriceNever below what the item really costs you plus your target, following your supplier's price.
StockTopped up while a supplier can supply; set to 0 only when every supplier definitely can't.
Handling timeMatched to how long your supplier takes to dispatch.

It follows your supplier's cost only. It never looks at competitors or the Buy Box. FBA listings are never touched.

1 · Read
Every 4 hours it reads each supplier page: price, postage, stock, delivery time.
→
2 · Work out
Pure maths: your real cost, Amazon's fee, VAT, your targets → the lowest safe price.
→
3 · Decide
Change the price? Go out of stock? Or hold because something is unclear?
→
4 · Send
Only if you armed that SKU and the master switch is on. Otherwise it just shows you.

2Adding a product (enrolling)

  • Every new product starts in dry run. It is tracked and priced on screen, but nothing is sent to Amazon until you arm it.
  • A listing that goes live on Amazon joins the Repricer by itself (in dry run). One you removed by hand stays out.
  • When a product joins, it gets your "new products start at" ROI/margin target and direction once. Changing those defaults later never re-prices products already tracked.
  • Supplier links: paste an eBay item link. Adding the same link twice does nothing.
  • eBay listings with sizes/colours: a link that names one variation (?var=) is read directly. A link without it is resolved for you when exactly one variation is in stock; if several are, you pick from a list. Old links saved before this rule need removing and adding again.
  • Sheets: download a supplier template already filled with every tracked SKU and its links, edit it, upload it back. A separate "min price" sheet lets you set minimum prices (and arm) in bulk.

3Reading your suppliers

  • How often: every 4 hours, or straight away with Check now.
  • What it reads from eBay: item price and currency, postage, whether it's in stock (yes / no / unknown), units left, the delivery window, carrier and seller.
  • Postage: the cheapest option; on a tie, the one that names a real carrier. eBay only quotes postage when told where it's going, so the app sends a postcode for your marketplace (for example B1 1AA for the UK, 10001 for the US).
  • "Calculated" postage is used only when eBay says which postcode it calculated to; otherwise postage is unknown — never treated as free.
  • Old readings (more than 24 hours) are not used.

4The numbers

Landed costSupplier price + supplier postage. If either is unknown, the landed cost is unknown.
Amazon's feeWhat Amazon actually took on this product's sold orders; else Amazon's fee quote; else your account's average. 15% only as a last resort.
VATFrom the account's setting. If it can't be read, nothing is priced that run.
Your own costsPostage label, an ads allowance and a flat minimum profit per unit — all £0.00 unless you set them.

The floor — the lowest price it will ever set

The floor is the highest of these, rounded up to the penny:

Break-even = (landed + label + ads + min profit) ÷ (k − fee rate)
ROI target = (landed + label + ads + landed × ROI%) ÷ (k − fee rate)
Margin target = (landed + label + ads) ÷ (k × (1 − margin%) − fee rate)
k = 1 with no VAT, or 1 ÷ 1.2 with 20% VAT

Worked example — supplier landed cost £12.00, Amazon fee 15%

No VAT20% VAT
Break-even (no profit)£14.12£17.57
20% return on your cash (ROI)£16.95£21.08
20% margin£18.47£23.23

Your own limits

  • Minimum price — your own number, independent of supplier readings. The price never goes below it, and a SKU cannot be armed without one.
  • Maximum price — if the floor (or your minimum) is above it, the listing goes out of stock rather than sell at a loss.
  • Hold price — an extra floor (for example the market price). If costs rise past it, the price rises above it.
  • Change alert (default 25%) — a bigger move still goes through; you are notified.
  • Small change (default £0.20) — smaller moves are skipped, except when the listing is below its floor, which is always lifted.
  • Stock — kept at 3 (or your default) while a supplier can supply.
  • Handling time = supplier's dispatch days − postage days (your setting, default 2) + any extra days you add. (Fixed 30 Sep: your postage-days setting was being ignored.)

5The decision for each product

OutcomeWhen
UpdateA usable supplier was chosen and the price, stock or handling time needs to change.
No changeAlready right (within the small-change amount).
Out of stockEvery supplier definitely can't supply: out of stock, ended (seen twice), too slow for your dispatch limit, switched off, or in the wrong currency. Also when your floor is above your maximum price.
HeldSomething is unclear, so it does nothing: a supplier unreadable or unknown, the Amazon price unknown, VAT unreadable, the listing gone from Amazon, FBA, or your targets can't be met. The screen says why.

Which supplier: the cheapest landed cost (default), the fastest, or your priority order.

6Direction: up only, up and down, or match the floor

Each SKU has a direction (default up only). In the example, a £12 supplier gives a floor of £16.95 (20% ROI, no VAT), and a £20 supplier gives £28.24.

Up only protects the price only — stock and handling time are still corrected. With a hold price of £24 on "up and down": a £12 supplier gives £24.00 (held); a £22 supplier gives £31.06 (the hold is exceeded).

7Dry run vs armed

A change is sent to Amazon only when all four are true:

1. Master switch onOff until you turn it on.
2. The SKU is armedGreen bolt on the row.
3. It has a minimum priceRequired to arm.
4. The decision is Update or Out of stockNot held.
  • Cooldown: at most one change per SKU every 4 hours (this includes going out of stock).
  • Changes are sent every 4 hours, or with Push now. Only one sending run can go at a time. (Fixed 30 Sep: two runs could overlap and send the same change twice.)
  • Disarm puts a SKU back in dry run. A SKU gone from Amazon is disarmed automatically.

8Exactly what is sent to Amazon

  • The app reads your listing from Amazon first, then changes only the fields below inside Amazon's own structure. It never rebuilds the listing.
  • Price — only if it differs by at least 1p.
  • Quantity and handling time — only if different, and handling time only where Amazon already has that field.
  • Out of stock sends quantity 0 and no price.
  • If Amazon refuses, it is recorded as rejected with Amazon's own words — never as a success.
  • The manual price box sends a price straight away (even in dry run), but never below your minimum price, and only the price.

13Questions

Why didn't my price drop when the supplier got cheaper?

The SKU is "up only" (the default). The row shows what the rules would have allowed. Switch the direction to "up and down" if you want it to follow cuts.

Why isn't it sending anything?

Check in this order: master switch on → SKU armed → minimum price set → the row's pause reason → the 4-hour cooldown.

Does a big price jump wait for me to approve it?

No. It goes through and you are notified (in the app and Slack).

Can it ever sell below my minimum price?

No — neither the automatic run nor the manual price box.

Does it look at competitors?

No. Only your supplier's cost and your own rules.

Can one account's Repricer change another account's listings?

No. Every read, rule and change is tied to one account and marketplace, using that account's own Amazon login.

How often does it run?

Suppliers are read every 4 hours; changes are sent every 4 hours; the deleted-listing check and fee refresh run daily.

14Decisions for you

These are how the Repricer behaves today. None is a bug, but you may want them different:

1. Wrong-currency supplier → out of stock. A USD supplier on a UK listing is refused; if it's the only supplier, the listing goes out of stock. Holding instead may be what you expect.
2. Stock stays 3 even if the supplier has only 1 left. The supplier's units-left figure is read but not used to cap your stock.
3. The 4-hour cooldown also delays going out of stock. A price sent at 10:00 and a supplier selling out at 11:00 means stock goes to 0 no earlier than 14:00.
4. Up only can keep a price above your maximum. If the listing already sells above your maximum price, up only leaves it there.
5. VAT is per account, not per marketplace. A VAT-registered UK account selling in the US gets US floors about 20% high.
6. Ads. The Repricer uses a flat ads allowance per unit (default £0), not the measured ad spend your Finance page uses.
7. One delivery postcode for the whole app, with no screen to set it. If set, it would also be used for US products.
